# Anchor schedules to a named time zone

Every Schedule will preserve its intended civil date and time together with a named Schedule Time Zone captured at creation or explicit edit. New schedules default to the current named device zone, while creation and edit flows allow an explicit alternative for future travel. A civil time skipped by an offset transition cannot be saved, while a repeated civil time requires the user to choose one of its represented offsets; that occurrence choice is preserved in the Schedule and backup. Device time-zone changes will trigger notification recalculation but will not move the commitment, and OnTime Backups will preserve the same zoned meaning across devices. Time-zone rules arrive only in app releases; after a rule update, future schedules retain their intended civil time while changed occurrence instants are calculated locally and disclosed to the user. Persisting a changed future occurrence and arming its new notification require explicit confirmation; past and completed schedules remain unchanged. The intended zoned time is the primary display; when the device is in another time zone, OnTime also shows the current-device equivalent and identifies the original zone in notification content. Treating schedules as floating device-local times was rejected because travel, daylight-saving changes, and cross-zone restore could silently change when a real commitment occurs. This adds a time-zone field and migration obligation but makes schedule and notification behavior deterministic and explainable offline.


## A10 clarification — calculation and application are separate

The A10 decision review distinguishes a device-zone change from a bundled time-zone-rule change. A device-zone change recalculates the home-day boundary and equivalent display; it does not rewrite the commitment, selected offset, or data revision. A rule change may produce a different future occurrence even though its civil fields are unchanged. OnTime calculates that difference automatically, shows the affected commitments, and applies the durable change only after explicit confirmation. Until then, the affected occurrence is not newly started or armed. An obsolete owned reservation is cancelled through the shared delivery owner; failed cancellation remains pending and cannot be replaced with a duplicate reservation.

An unknown zone, a repeated time without a selected occurrence, or a skipped time never silently becomes UTC or the current device zone. Valid future civil fields with one represented occurrence can be resolved without inventing a device offset. Past explicit offsets and frozen or started occurrences retain their recorded meaning; uncertain past offsets are not replaced with guessed history. Changing a Schedule Time Zone explicitly retains its civil fields and requires choosing and confirming the new occurrence. Recurring expansion's existing gap, count, selected-offset, slot, override, and exclusion rules are unchanged.

Home “today” uses the current device civil day's half-open instant interval, whose duration can differ from 24 hours. Month-calendar placement retains the commitment's original zone civil date. Comparisons, overlap, ordering, and countdown use resolved occurrence instants. The original zoned commitment remains the primary display, with the current-device equivalent when it differs.

Civil values must be represented without the device's DST normalization. An internal UTC carrier, if used, carries wall fields and is not itself the commitment instant. Existing civil TEXT and backup-format semantics are retained; this is not an instruction to rewrite stored civil values as UTC instants. Restore corrections happen in staging, followed by complete validation and a new verified preview before final apply. Cancelling that flow changes no active data.

These are accepted A10 requirements from four recorded root/delegate questions in `plans/audit-2026-09-23/issues/A10.md`; they do not claim that the existing consumers or staging UI already implement them.


## A10 implementation clarification — explicit correction preserves protection

An active-store time-correction command uses the same generation, incarnation, version and idempotent mutation receipt as the aggregate writer. Inside the transaction it re-derives the typed unknown, ambiguous or stale time problem from the current baseline; a client-supplied error label is not authority. The narrow correction is available only for an unstarted, unfrozen, incomplete occurrence whose original instant is not provably past. A passed preparation-start threshold alone may be corrected explicitly only when both the newly chosen occurrence and its preparation start are after the final current UTC instant. This does not expose a generic bypass for name, preparation or place edits and does not invent elapsed time, a run or an outcome. Actual started, frozen, completed and provably historical commitments keep their protection.

The confirmed future recurrence split and retained closed-segment reference contract is defined in ADR0028. Active changes use the aggregate baseline and mutation authority; staging never invokes an active-store writer. A committed correction whose old owned notification cannot yet be cancelled remains committed with pending delivery cleanup, under the existing single-owner replacement rules. It is not silently rolled back or reported as fully delivered. Device-zone movement updates equivalent display and day boundaries without changing durable occurrence or revision. These additional decisions are recorded in `plans/audit-2026-09-23/a10-implementation-review.md` and are not a claim of implementation or test completion.

### Capture a previously unselected occurrence at its actual first start

Reads, countdowns, form loading and notification reconciliation do not persist an inferred offset. A successful first-start command is a narrower boundary: inside the same write transaction it rechecks the current row, protection and closed-reference status, loaded rules and final UTC time. Only a known-zone, unique occurrence that is not already past may have its rule-derived offset recorded together with its first `startedAt` and frozen preparation. This records the actual start's target; it does not copy the device offset. A partial write rolls back the entire change, and a retry preserves the original target and start receipt. An already started/frozen legacy row with a null offset, or an unknown, ambiguous, nonexistent, stale or closed-tail occurrence, cannot use this path as an implicit repair.

The execution fingerprint represents the same resolved instant and original civil, zone and preparation meaning before and after that atomic null-to-selected-offset transition. It must not invalidate the same run merely because the raw offset was recorded. An actual target change changes the interpretation and requires the existing notification-owner reconciliation rules. The global rule-data identity remains separate review/claim authority; an unrelated rule update must not invalidate a historical run whose target is already frozen. This clarification addresses restart and lateness after the target time passes, when an unrecorded historical null offset would otherwise lose its justified target. The additional question, answer and validation evidence belong in `plans/audit-2026-09-23/a10-implementation-followups.md`; acceptance of the decision does not claim its implementation is complete.

The not-already-past condition applies to capturing a previously null offset. It does not introduce a blanket ban on an otherwise permitted manual late start with an explicit, preserved offset. Keep the existing start, completion, restore-confirmation and recurrence-retention gates. A runtime interpretation is an immutable view created with one fixed evaluation time; countdowns, timing identity and preparation start use that same interpretation without repeated ambient-clock reads. It does not mutate the stored row or itself authorize a start. Final execution still rechecks current row/version, generation, loaded rules and the final time at the existing command boundary. Modal date, time, zone and recurrence responses also retain the A08 form-owner authority: a delayed response from an older draft cannot edit or validate a replacement draft.

### Preserve strict legacy runtime compatibility when changing the civil carrier

The former database converter constructed a local `DateTime`, so its legacy fingerprint serializer emitted civil text without `Z`. A UTC civil carrier must reproduce those exact wall fields for the existing A11 in-memory legacy comparison; appending `Z` merely because the carrier changed would invalidate unchanged progress. Keep complete fingerprint and preparation-step checks. Do not parse arbitrary legacy fingerprint fragments, remove timezone text indiscriminately, or force-match values whose civil meaning actually changed through prior device normalization. A fixed historical literal read against the current SQLite row is the regression fixture, not a fingerprint generated by the new serializer under test.

A typed unresolved time is an item-specific interpretation problem, not evidence that the entire encrypted database is unavailable. During runtime privacy migration, preserve that schedule and its durable facts, clear only the unverifiable installation-owned transient projection, and allow valid independent items to migrate. The unresolved item gains no automatic start, completion or alarm authority. Actual database, runtime-storage or cleanup failures retain their failure behavior and cannot produce a successful migration marker. These rules do not claim that all migration and native-device checks have passed.

The domain entity owns only the immutable time interpretation value, status, occurrence and typed unresolved exception. Loaded timezone lookup and rule interpretation remain in the adapter. The factory that combines a stored schedule with preparation requires an explicitly supplied fixed-pass interpretation; it must not consult the device clock or registry implicitly. A direct explicit-offset value remains sufficient for pure domain calculations. This preserves the domain dependency boundary and makes the instant used before and after asynchronous preparation loading identical within the read pass. Architecture tests remain unchanged.

### Refresh recommendations without inventing or replacing an active run

An upcoming recommendation has a bounded future query and must be reevaluated when time alone changes eligibility. Device midnight, foreground device-day/zone changes and resume renew that query; obsolete asynchronous preparation reads cannot replace a newer selection. Timer wake-up granularity is separate from civil/instant precision: exact comparisons remain intact while sub-millisecond wake-ups use a nonzero supported timer delay.

The existing materialized-store stream also supplies durable incomplete runs identified by `isStarted`, `startedAt` and frozen preparation, even when their original target lies outside the future date window. This does not expand historical recurrence or promote partial legacy markers. Completed/deleted rows lose this eligibility through authoritative store changes. A validated runtime projection may continue its original run; a missing projection cannot be replaced with invented elapsed time or an automatic new start.

The actual routed home uses the device's half-open day for the today card. An incomplete durable run may remain accessible outside today under an explicit in-progress heading; an ordinary tomorrow recommendation is not presented as today's commitment. On date/zone/resume changes the home refreshes today and equivalent displays. A calendar still in its initial automatic today mode follows a new current month, while explicit user month/day navigation is preserved. Earlier tests of the unused weekly home do not constitute evidence for this routed monthly home. These are recorded A10 follow-up decisions; implementation and regression completion remain separately tracked.


A closed recurring reference forbids a new automatic start, but does not erase an existing incomplete durable Preparation Run. Nearest/home access may retain a reference with `isStarted`, a recorded `startedAt` and frozen preparation. Resuming its timer still requires a validated runtime snapshot whose start instant agrees with the durable run; a durable marker alone is not authority to synthesize elapsed progress. Missing or inconsistent runtime requires explicit recovery/confirmation and must not cause a background start write or navigation. Completed/deleted rows cease to qualify. Pending work from a replaced nearest subscription cannot overwrite the newer query's result.


Time-correction conflict review checks all provable resolved instant/preparation overlaps within the existing bounded proof contract. Unresolved history/completed or closed non-active records do not receive new execution authority and do not block unrelated resolved corrections. An unresolved future/active item that might intersect the reviewed range is disclosed separately as an uncertain impact, using conservative supported civil/offset/preparation bounds; unavailable bounds cannot prove disjointness. It is never described as conflict-free. Correcting that item later requires renewed conflict validation, and its unresolved status cannot grant a new start/alarm. This separation permits active-store corrections and individual authenticated staging edits; it does not grant a staged candidate verified-preview or activation authority while unresolved future requirements remain. Original validated active-run access/completion is preserved under its own authority.


A failed nearest/preparation read is not evidence that a Schedule was deleted. The last validated visible run is retained with an explicit retry state; failed date/store inputs cannot reuse stale cached authority, and no new automatic or explicit start may derive from that failed projection. A later authoritative missing/completed result still follows normal cleanup. A pending read or snapshot restoration cannot erase a newer error/result. Restoring a validated existing run is distinct from a new-start event in the actual home navigation listener, not only in the navigation service. These are the accepted read-failure follow-up semantics; focused and complete-source execution results remain separately recorded.

### Authenticated restore of historical records with removed zone names

A bounded schema-valid zone name missing from current rule data does not require rewriting a certainly historical or completed commitment's original civil fields, selected offset or facts. Preserve it with an explicit historical-zone uncertainty explanation; preserve a null historical offset as uncertainty too. This preservation grants no new start or alarm authority. An actual durable started/frozen Preparation Run is identified as a protected run, not mislabeled as a completed historical event; access/completion and restore confirmation remain subject to their existing runtime authority. A frozen flag alone cannot classify a future unstarted record as historical or waive the unresolved-future activation requirement. Future unstarted unknown-zone commitments still require explicit known-zone correction before verified preview/apply. Malformed scalar types, dates, offsets, IDs and graph ownership remain hard failures. This decision is for the staged repair implementation under construction and does not claim completed validation.

The portable representation does not persist live `isStarted`; its decoder restores that flag as false. Therefore portable preservation relies on the existing strictly validated `startedAt` plus frozen-preparation pair as a **protected start fact**, not a claim of an active runtime. Do not require a non-persisted live flag to preserve that fact. Actual runtime resume authority is established separately after restore through explicit confirmation and matching validation. The future frozen-only case still has no exemption. This corrects the initial live-store wording for the portable boundary.
